# WTC II, no. 4: Fugue in C-sharp minor, BWV 873

Three voices, 71 bars, 12/16. The page counts four dotted-quaver beats per bar; internal times are crotchets. The source is Kroll's Bach-Gesellschaft text, encoded by David Huron and revised by Craig Sapp. The Humdrum encoding is unchanged.

## Analysis and source comparison

The five formal groups follow Keller, pp. 132–133: exposition with episode (1–15), second group (16–23), inverted group and direct return (24–34), second theme and episode (35–47), and combinations (48–71). These groups are not five uninterrupted expositions. Tovey, p. 21, recognises the second subject and its subsequent combinations; Prout, pp. 57–58, treats bars 31–47 as a long episode. Keller regards the double-fugue classification as borderline because the chromatic theme is foreshadowed earlier and immediately accompanied by material derived from the first subject.

The opening real answer transposes the 22 pitches of the bass subject by a perfect twelfth (19 semitones, a fifth plus an octave). The terminal note is shortened and runs into a sequential extension. Prout places the end of the answer at the first semiquaver of bar 4; this study follows the longer model illustrated by Keller, through the returning G sharp at bar 4 beat 2. The displayed model includes the first returning tonic and its tie through bar 3 beat 1. Later complete direct entries normally end at the corresponding tonic, with sequential extensions labelled separately. The entries at 48 and 61 alter the continuation substantially; they are marked as variants rather than mechanically extended copies of the model. At bar 55 E sharp temporarily replaces the minor third. The last answer alters several intervals.

There are 16 selected first-subject statements: bass 1; soprano 2:3; alto 5; soprano 16; alto 17:3; bass 20; inverted soprano 24, alto 26 and bass 28; direct alto 30; soprano 48; altered inverted alto 53; bass 55; alto 61 and 66; bass 67:3. The three central inversions reverse diatonic contour exactly, with chromatic intervals adjusted to the harmony. The inversion at 53 is freer and is labelled accordingly. Prout's final-section prose omits the combination at 61, discussed explicitly by Keller and Tovey; it is included here.

The second theme's model begins in the soprano at 35. The alto enters at 36:1 with its initial B displaced down an octave for the first dotted quaver, then returns to the upper octave; the continuation is incomplete. The bass begins on the last beat of 37, the upbeat to Keller's bar 38, with a shortened opening. Subsequent combinations pair the themes at 48, 55, 61, 66 and 67–68. Their continuations vary and are explicitly marked. The final alto statement at 68 compresses the last two notes in bar 70. Its entry follows the bass answer by half a bar, so the last pair is not aligned at the themes' beginnings.

The subject starts at 48:1, 55:1 and 67:3 fall on tied continuations, not fresh attacks. The diagram marks the metrical beginning of the subject pattern; playback preserves the earlier attack and joins the tie. The first-subject return at 48 begins on C sharp over F-sharp minor, and the entry at 61 begins on G sharp over C-sharp minor. The harmonic markers distinguish these sonorities from the subjects' starting pitches.

There is no separately coloured fixed countersubject: Keller finds no independent counterpoint of comparable significance; Tovey calls the simple early accompaniment a fairly regular countersubject but distinguishes it from the later second subject. Chromatic foreshadowings are marked ch, not counted as additional second-subject entries. The six-note sequential tail t is identified by exact diatonic transposition or inversion, with uninterrupted semiquaver rhythm. The arpeggio-and-scale figure r is marked at selected clear statements; its altered forms at 33–34 carry an asterisk. These marks identify recurring material, not every passing resemblance.

Keller proposes readings from the earlier C minor version at bars 26, 41 and 70. Those are not silently substituted into Kroll's transmitted C-sharp minor text. The last chord contains E sharp, lasts one dotted quaver and is followed by three beats of rests. Tovey's performance comments specifically emphasise the short ending.

## Reproduction and verification

Run `prepare_score.py`, `analyse_materials.py`, `engrave.py`, `prepare_pdf_images.mjs`, then `build_pdfs.py --prout FULL_PROUT_SCAN.pdf --tovey FULL_TOVEY_BOOK_II_SCAN.pdf`. Python requires Verovio 6.3, ReportLab and pypdf; image conversion uses the project's Sharp dependency. Intermediate files go in `tmp/wtc-ii-04` at the repository root.

The source parser independently checks every pitch and onset against Verovio. Both staff arrangements retain all 1,443 written notes and 90 ties; synthetic playback has 1,353 sounding events. `check_analysis.py` checks real-answer transposition, the three central diatonic inversions, late tied-in openings, second-theme combinations, chromatic variants, annotation bounds and the final chord and silence. The collection tests include dotted-quaver beat fractions. Low alto passages use bass clef in the open score and move to the lower stave in the keyboard arrangement; voice identity and all note events are preserved.

The companion extracts preserve Prout's printed pp. 57–58 and Tovey's printed p. 21, including adjoining commentary, with links to their complete scans. Keller remains an external link. NBS playback uses Christine Schornsheim's official performance, starting at 4:29, as specified by the NBS chapter marker; it is not synchronised bar by bar.
