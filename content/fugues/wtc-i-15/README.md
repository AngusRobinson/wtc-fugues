# WTC I, no. 15: Fugue in G major, BWV 860

Three voices, 86 bars, 6/8. Printed beats are dotted crotchets; machine-readable times are crotchets. The score is Kroll’s Bach-Gesellschaft text, encoded by David Huron and revised by Craig Sapp. The original Humdrum file is preserved unchanged.

## Analytical decisions

The real answer is an exact transposition down a fourth. The two rising melodic leaps are sevenths; Keller’s seventh and ninth refer to their destinations above the dominant. Subject spans include the closing quaver at the beginning of the fifth bar. The exposition gives the subject to soprano, alto and bass at 1, 5 and 11. The inverted counter-exposition gives it to alto, soprano and bass at 20, 24 and 28; the first two endings are adjusted and the bass omits the last bar. The E minor pair comprises the direct soprano entry at 38 and the incomplete alto inversion at 43.

Keller and Tovey locate the first stretto at 51; Prout includes the opening of 51 within the preceding episode. The score has soprano at 51:1⅙ and bass at 52:1. Both omit the subject’s second bar; the bass also lacks the last bar. The leading alto at 60:2 has the same omission as the soprano at 51, but reaches the closing note. The responding soprano at 61:2 gives the head and an adapted continuation without either leap-bar. The inversion at 69:2 curtails the ending. The final group at 77–81 includes incomplete inverted entries and a freely continued direct statement a third above the original pitch. The alto’s fragment in thirds from 79 is marked separately, without treating it as a complete new entry. The bass at 78 starts one semiquaver after the bar-line and sequences the head.

Eight countersubject spans distinguish the model at 6:2 from abridged or adapted recurrences. The opening of the alto at 12:2 is rhythmically altered. Inversions at 21:2, 25:2, 29:2 and 44:2 omit the countersubject’s final bar; the alto at 40 omits its first half-bar. At 70 the inverted countersubject starts with a semiquaver rest, which is excluded from its coloured span. Freely accompanying material is not labelled as a further countersubject.

The triple counterpoint is identified explicitly by Tovey and described through its permutations by Keller. The three lines are named p (rising third and descending continuation), q (slower line), and r (semiquaver figure), avoiding confusion between Keller’s different uses of letter labels. Their soprano–alto–bass dispositions are p–q–r at 17–19, r–q–p at 31–33, q–p–r at 48–50, and r–p–q at 65–67. The slower line is detached at 31 and varied again at 65; the latter episode continues through 68. The semiquaver figure originates in the codetta. Clear diatonic/rhythmic recurrences and inversions are marked separately; d identifies the scale passages at 34–37 and 73–76.

The five headings above the diagram are editorial groupings based on Keller’s account of the entry groups, rather than an attributed five-part formal scheme. Harmonic markers distinguish cadences, other arrivals and entry context. In particular, the inverted bass at 69 starts on a bare D octave, not a tonic chord. The first-inversion arrivals at 20 and 51 are not labelled cadences.
