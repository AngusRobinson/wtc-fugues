# WTC fugue analyses: style guide

## Audience and language

Write for an advanced undergraduate musician. Use concise analytical prose and British English: bars, crotchets, quavers, semiquavers, minims and staves. Avoid promotional headings, slogans, “Listen for”, explanations of standard musical terms and redundant colour-key instructions. Keep the public README about the project, not its construction. Public pages and downloads must contain no personal details, conversation history or production notes.

## Analysis and terminology

- Begin with the subject and countersubjects. Identify the opening answer explicitly as **tonal** or **real**, both in the heading and its first description; explain a significant tonal alteration precisely.
- Label recurring accompanying themes as countersubjects, with “main countersubject” where useful. Use *counterpoint* as an abstract noun. Use *double* or *triple counterpoint* where the combination is demonstrably invertible. Describe contrapuntal inversion as inversion, not merely an exchange of voices.
- Where there is no regular countersubject, say so and identify recurring derived figures. Distinguish full entries, altered or incomplete entries and head allusions.
- Define motif letters by their full names at their first mention in the written disposition. Use explicit voice names and bar/beat positions there; avoid compressed strings whose meaning depends on deciphering arrows.
- Show the overall formal divisions above the overview diagram. Include the exposition and subsequent entry groups, stretti and episodes as appropriate to the work; do not impose a single formal scheme on every fugue.
- Distinguish harmonic arrivals and cadences from the key context of an entry. A dominant chord or a subject entry alone does not establish a modulation. Place markers at the actual notated beat, including arrivals within a bar.
- Roman numerals refer to the fugue's home key, with case reflecting the major or minor key/chord named by the marker. Keep the analytical event data consistent across the diagram, browser score and PDFs.

## Sources and verification

Make an independent reading of the score, check Keller and Prout, and consult Tovey where available. Prefer Keller when interpretations remain in doubt, but verify his bar references and claims against the score. Record material differences of reading or voice allocation briefly in the source details.

Keep source discussion short on the page. Link Keller's hosted text; provide credited public-domain Prout and Tovey extracts where available. Credit the score edition, David Huron and Craig Sapp, and retain source notices. Do not reproduce Keller's text or a translation without an appropriate basis for doing so.

Verify entry pitches and onsets, answer alterations, thematic spans, voice assignments, tonal arrivals, ties and closing chords. Label editorial judgements as such. Do not silently alter the underlying musical reading to solve a layout problem.

## Browser page

Use this order: compact thematic examples; overview diagram with formal headings; contrapuntal disposition, open by default; full score. Keep examples at an appropriate engraving scale rather than stretching short excerpts across the page. Show substantial stretches of the score at once.

Place compact key/Roman-numeral labels above the exact harmonic-arrival beats in the full score. Keep labels aligned when resizing, zooming or scrolling; separate crowded labels without losing their anchors. Do not collect them at the right end of a system. Entry-only key-context markers belong in the overview diagram.

Retain the NBS recording where available and synthetic playback as an unobtrusive alternative. Do not autoplay. Do not imply that the recording follows the score cursor without implemented synchronisation. Avoid extra instructions around the player.

## Engraving and PDFs

Provide annotated open-score and playable two-stave PDFs. Preserve notes, durations, ties and the selected edition's readings in both formats.

- Omit voice prefixes such as “A:” from score annotations; their position identifies the part.
- Use conventional cross-shaped double sharps. Retain rests needed to distinguish parts; omit redundant whole-bar rests in shared staves.
- Set stems, rests and ties according to the actual musical parts and their vertical disposition, including crossings. Check tie curves against notes, accidentals and other ties, including continuations over line breaks; bounding boxes alone are insufficient.
- Keep the opening title and colour key, omit running headers, and print the source credit once at the end. Use plain page numbers without a denominator.
- In PDFs, number only the first bar of each system. Put harmonic-arrival Roman numerals above the top stave at their precise beats. Omit entry-only key-context markers.
- Use economical, readable spacing. Preserve the four-page Book I C-sharp minor keyboard layout unless a substantive change requires otherwise.

Render and inspect every affected PDF page, with dense passages, endings and reported defects at readable size. Check bar numbers, Roman numerals, thematic labels, rests, accidentals and ties for collisions. Verify browser placement at desktop and mobile widths and after changing score size. Rebuild and check the collection before delivery.

## Collection structure

Use the shared components and per-fugue catalogue/data structure so studies remain consistent across the planned 48 fugues. Keep internal links compatible with GitHub Pages at either a repository subdirectory or a custom domain. Update the local collection and download packages with each completed change; publish only when requested.
